# Recruitment plan and messages

The target is 150–300 completed responses. This population is unusually
reachable — PG residents and hostel students are already organised into WhatsApp
groups, and small landlords into owners' associations and listing groups — so the
constraint is not finding people but writing an ask they will act on.

---

## Rules every message below follows

**Never name the product, and never describe it.** Section 6 of the instrument
asks about rental apps in general, and Q16 carries the project's central claim. A
respondent who has been told "I'm building a rent app, please fill my survey"
knows which answer helps, and a meaningful proportion will supply it. The product
is mentioned nowhere in the recruitment, the form title, or the thank-you page.
This costs a little goodwill and buys the only thing that makes the data worth
collecting.

**Never state what previous respondents chose.** "300 people have already
responded and most said yes" biases every person who reads it, through plain
social proof. It is also the sentence a methods examiner looks for first.

**Always state the time cost, and be right about it.** "Five minutes" that turns
out to be twelve produces abandoned forms. Time the form yourself before sending
anything.

**Always say what is not collected.** "It doesn't ask for your name or number" is
the single line that most increases completion among people who have been burned
by forms that harvest contacts.

**Ask once, remind once.** A third message annoys people and converts nobody.

---

## Where to send, and what to expect

Rough conversion rates — plan with these rather than hoping.

| Channel | Who | Realistic yield | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Your own class / year WhatsApp groups | Student tenants | 15–25% of members | Highest trust, send first |
| PG and hostel resident groups | Tenants | 8–15% | Ask the group admin before posting |
| College-wide or department groups | Mixed | 3–8% | Volume compensates |
| Instagram story with a link sticker | Mixed | 1–3% of viewers | Cheap; re-post once at a different hour |
| Owners' / landlord association groups | Landlords | 5–12% | The hard side; see below |
| Property listing groups (local Facebook / WhatsApp) | Landlords | 2–5% | Admin permission needed |
| One-to-one asks | Either | 50–70% | Reserve for the landlord shortfall |

**The landlord side is the one that will fall short.** Tenants are organised into
groups and landlords mostly are not, so expect the tenant branch to fill in days
and the landlord branch to stall around 20–40. Two things fix it: ask your own
landlord and your friends' landlords directly (one-to-one asks convert at over
half), and ask one owners'-association admin to forward the message rather than
posting it yourself — a forward from the admin reads as endorsed.

If the landlord count lands under about 30, report tenant results as the main
finding and landlord results as indicative, with the count stated beside every
landlord percentage. A small sample described honestly is fine. A small sample
described as though it were large is not.

**Timing.** Weekday evenings between 7pm and 10pm, or Sunday mornings. Avoid the
first three days of the month, when rent is actually being paid and chased, and
avoid exam weeks for student groups.

## Message 1 — Your own class or year group (send first)

> Hi all — I'm doing a short survey for my postgraduate project on how rent,
> deposits and maintenance get recorded in PGs, hostels and rented flats.
>
> It's 5 minutes, anonymous, and doesn't ask for your name or number.
>
> If you're renting right now, it would really help me out: [LINK]
>
> Thanks 🙏

---

## Message 2 — PG / hostel resident group

*Ask the admin first. A message posted without permission gets deleted and may get
you removed.*

> Hi everyone — I'm a postgraduate student doing research on how rent and deposits
> are recorded in PGs and hostels — receipts, agreements, deposit returns, that
> sort of thing.
>
> It's a 5-minute survey. It's anonymous and doesn't ask for your name, number or
> address. I'm not selling anything and this isn't for a company.
>
> If you're renting at the moment: [LINK]
>
> Happy to share what I find with the group once it's done.

*That last line is worth keeping. It is the main thing you can offer in return,
and it costs you nothing to honour.*

---

## Message 3 — Message to a group admin, asking permission

> Hi — I'm a postgraduate student at [university]. I'm running a short academic
> survey on how rent and deposits are recorded in rented accommodation.
>
> Would you be okay with me posting it in the group, or would you prefer to forward
> it yourself? It's 5 minutes, anonymous, collects no contact details, and isn't
> commercial.
>
> Happy to send you the questions first so you can see exactly what's being asked.

*Offer the questions, and mean it. Admins say yes far more often when they can
check, and it takes one message.*

---

## Message 4 — Landlord / owners' association group

Landlords respond to a different framing. They are not interested in helping with
coursework, and they are interested in what other owners do.

> Hello — I'm a postgraduate student researching how rent collection and records
> are handled in independently owned rental properties.
>
> I'm looking for owners who let out anywhere from one room to twenty-plus. The
> survey takes about 4 minutes, is anonymous, and asks nothing about your income or
> your tenants' details.
>
> [LINK]
>
> I'll share a summary of the findings with the group afterwards — it should be
> useful to see how other owners handle arrears and receipts.

## Message 5 — One-to-one ask (highest conversion — use for the landlord gap)

> Hi [name] — could I ask a favour? I'm doing a survey for my PG project on how
> rent and deposits get recorded. It's about 4 minutes and completely anonymous —
> no name, no number.
>
> [LINK]
>
> Would really help. And if you know one or two other owners who might fill it,
> I'd be grateful if you could pass it on.

---

## Message 6 — Instagram story / WhatsApp status

Keep it to two lines; a story is read in under two seconds.

> Doing a 5-min survey for my PG project on renting — rent, deposits, receipts.
> Anonymous, no name or number asked. Link in sticker 👇

## Message 7 — Printed notice for a PG noticeboard

Generate a QR code for the form link and put it on an A5 sheet.

> **5-minute anonymous survey**
>
> Postgraduate research on how rent, deposits and repairs are recorded in PGs and
> hostels.
>
> No name, no phone number, no address collected.
>
> *[QR code]*
>
> Thank you — [your first name], [university]

---

## Message 8 — The single reminder (3 days after the original)

Send once, and only in groups where you posted the original.

> Quick reminder about the renting survey I posted — it's still open and takes
> about 5 minutes. Thanks to everyone who's already filled it in.
>
> [LINK]

*Note what this does not say: not how many have responded, not what they chose,
not that you are short of responses. "Thanks to everyone who's already filled it
in" acknowledges them without quantifying anything.*

## Before you send a single message

1. **Fill the form yourself, twice** — once down the tenant branch, once down the
   landlord branch. Check the branching sends you to the right sections and that
   the attention check behaves.
2. **Time it.** If it runs over 6 minutes, cut questions until it doesn't. Q11,
   Q14, Q17 and Q22 are the optional ones to cut first.
3. **Delete every test response** before the first real message goes out. Open the
   response sheet and confirm it is empty. A test row left in the sheet becomes a
   data point nobody can identify later.
4. **Check the form accepts responses from people not signed in.** The commonest
   failure is a form restricted to one organisation, which silently turns away
   most of the people you are recruiting.
5. **Send to one small group first** and watch the first ten responses arrive. If
   something is confusing, you find out at a cost of ten responses rather than
   three hundred.
